## 4. Occupied, residual gap, meaning
- **Occupied.** The concepts of watermark stealing, spoofing and scrubbing and their evaluation protocols (token level); exact score tests for weight perturbations (GaussMark); context hashing and key rotation as mechanisms, and their robustness–security trade-offs (KGW, Reliability, No Free Lunch, Learnability, SynthID-Text, SEEK); the observation that recovering the vector allows spoofing and the rotating-vector idea (Self-Recognition, future work); per-document keyed activation steering with metadata (SLAM); keyed activation directions for monitoring (AWM); the fact that trained probes on activations can be fooled by paraphrased text (Self-Recognition's own observation).
- **Residual gap (established by this gate at full-text level, search level for absence).** No prior work measures, for any activation-steering watermark: key recovery from outputs and its sample cost; forgery at a fixed false-positive rate at matched fluency; scrubbing at matched quality under an exact test; an exact per-key-calibrated test for the family; or the stealing–robustness–quality cost of context-keyed or rotating directions. Type: an adequacy (security) check of a published method family, plus a transferred statistical tool, plus an adequacy check of a transferred defence. Not a new method.
- **Is it meaningful?** Yes. A deployer of Self-Recognition-style steering learns that (i) the published detector type is forgeable without the key at the only strength where it is quality-neutral; (ii) an exact key test rescues detection and blocks passive forgery but is itself the thief's estimator, so the fixed key is forgeable from 64 texts; (iii) keying by context stops that thief at the budget tested but gives up paraphrase robustness, which is the family's motivation; (iv) rotation buys only a constant factor. The regulator texts (§8) ask for exactly these measurements: error rates, robustness to paraphrase and adversarial attacks, and security against forged watermarks.

**PASS with narrowed wording** for C1–C10 and C13; C11 and C12 are leads or inferences and are labelled. - **Code of Practice on marking and labelling of AI-generated content (final, 10 June 2026; voluntary; 38 pp; `ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/129555`):** Sub-measure 1.1.2: "Signatories will ensure that AI-generated or manipulated content is marked with an imperceptible watermark, with the exception of very short text. For free-form text longer than 200 tokens, watermarking still needs to be applied, even though it may have lower reliability compared to that of watermarking very long text." and "The imperceptible watermark will be embedded within the content in a manner that is difficult for it to be separated from the content. The watermark is intended to serve as a robust mechanism to complement the digitally signed metadata" (p. 9). Measure 2.1: free access to the detection solution for "competent market surveillance authorities and other regulators, law enforcement authorities, media, fact-checkers, trusted flaggers, independent researchers, educational and research institutions, and civil society organisations" (p. 13); access to text-watermark detection "may" be restricted "to the extent that they have a lower level of reliability and robustness and that they may produce misleading or low-confidence results" (p. 13). Measure 3.2 Reliability: "the capability of the marking and detection solutions to accurately identify and distinguish the origin of AI-generated or manipulated content from other content", measured by "the error rate of the detection" on "both content generated or manipulated by their AI system with their own marking and other content" (p. 17). Measure 3.3 Robustness: solutions must "maintain intended performance levels under varying conditions, covering both common alterations and adversarial attacks" (p. 17); typical operations include "lexical substitution, homoglyphs" and "paraphrasing, translation cycles" (p. 18); "Signatories will choose the considered malicious behaviour as plausible real-world threats based on the type of content, the type of mark and the context of deployment" and "are encouraged to apply standard cybersecurity practices, such as rate limits, to prevent and counteract malicious use and attacks against their marking and detection solutions" (p. 18). Forensic detection (Measure 2.2) "were not deemed mature enough" (p. 15).
- **Commission Guidelines on Article 50 (20 July 2026; 51 pp; document 131215):** Recital 133's examples "include watermarks, metadata identifications, cryptographic methods for proving provenance and authenticity of content, logging methods, fingerprints or other techniques, and a combination of such techniques" (p. 25); at the initial stage "for watermarking technologies, the provider may rely on its own detection solution or on a third party or shared detection solution" (p. 26).
- **NIST AI 100-4, "Reducing Risks Posed by Synthetic Content" (Nov 2024; PDF pages):** definitions: "Robust: The watermark should remain detectable under various types of typical innocuous modifications such as ... minor paraphrases or deletions (for text)"; "Secure: The watermark should be secure against attempts by malicious users to remove or tamper with the watermark information, or to insert forged watermarks." (p. 13). "Malicious actors may also try to forge, or 'spoof,' a watermark, embedding a falsified signal about the history of the content. Spoofing could undermine watermarks' usefulness: if a substantial amount of non-watermarked content registers as watermarked ... users may come to disregard the watermarks as a meaningful signal" (p. 19). "Scaling is particularly difficult for private watermarking schemes, where the detection algorithms, keys, or machine learning models necessary for detection are kept private. In such a setting, the entity holding the detection tools must be trusted" (p. 20). Evaluation should cover "robustness and security against different kinds of innocuous modifications and adversarial attacks. The latter may include removal attacks (i.e., removing the watermark without having to break the encryption or obtain the watermarking key); distorting watermarks to fool a detector; cracking security measures to remove the watermark; and forgery attempts." (p. 38).
- **Industry:** SynthID-Text (Nature 2024) and its Hugging Face configuration (§3); the ETH SRI probing blog (§3).
