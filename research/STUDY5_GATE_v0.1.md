# Source gate v0.1 — Study 5: a context-keyed or rotating-key defence for activation-steering watermarks

Date: 2026-10-02. **Stage 5 (Study 5 design), Milestone 8.**
**Status: CONDITIONAL → PASS as a transfer (condition met 2026-10-02; §7).** The defence idea is a **transfer** of two token-level mechanisms (context hashing with width *h*; rotation among *K* keys) to activation steering. Its stealability, paraphrase-robustness and quality trade-off has not been measured for any activation-level scheme, and the target scheme's own authors name it as future work. The gap depends on one unresolved question: **whether there is a key-recovery attack worth defending against at the budget studied** (§1 and §5: the single next check). Search absence is not proof of novelty.

## 1. The candidate, in one sentence
*Decision:* whether the owner of a Self-Recognition-style watermark should make the steering direction depend on a secret **and** on the text's own context — per token, v_t = f(secret, x_{t−h..t−1}) (context hashing of width *h*), or per text, one of *K* secret keys (rotation) — so that an attacker holding the open model and *n* watermarked outputs cannot recover the direction, while the owner's exact key test, which re-derives the per-position keys from the text (or tests the union of the *K* keys), still detects genuine text after paraphrase, at a measured quality cost.
*Information set:* the attacker has the open weights, *n* outputs and the scheme's public parameters (*h* or *K*, the sparsity; the layer unknown), not the secret. The owner's detector sees the text only: no prompt, no per-text metadata.
*Targets:* (i) key recovery against *n*, now per context or per key; (ii) forgery acceptance under the exact test at a fixed false-positive rate, at matched fluency; (iii) genuine detection before and after paraphrase at matched quality (Study 2's pipeline); (iv) the perplexity and repetition cost against the undefended watermark at the same strength ρ.
*Comparator:* the fixed-key scheme of Studies 1–3 (*h* = 0, *K* = 1) on the same keys, strengths and prompts.
*Failure mechanism addressed:* a fixed direction is a consistent first-order drift in the re-encoded gradient of every output, so averaging over outputs estimates it (Study 3's leakage lens: forgeries pass where the recovered cosine is high). Context keying removes the consistency across outputs (per text) or across positions (per token): the attacker must estimate one direction per context from the positions sharing it, which multiplies the sample cost by the number of contexts, while the owner, who re-derives the context from the text, loses nothing **unless an edit changes the context**. That is exactly the robustness cost to measure.

**The premise that needs checking.**
- *Evidence.* Study 1's Route A, an estimator on mean re-encoded activations, **fails against the fixed key** at every strength at *n* ≤ 1,024: the median cos(v̂, v) over 8 keys is 0.160, 0.074, 0.151 and 0.015 at ρ = 0.25, 0.35, 0.50 and 0.70 (`outputs/study1_v0.2/results.json`; rule R1 "fails at this budget" at every level; the layer is identified for at most 3 of 8 keys). Under the exact test (Study 3), its forgeries pass for a few keys only: key 1004 is recovered at cos 0.71–0.93 and its forgeries are accepted 97–100% (v0.4, *n* = 256, ρ = 0.50 and 0.70); key 1003 at ρ = 0.50 reaches cos 0.48 and 67% (v0.2, *n* = 1,024); the other six keys stay at cos ≤ 0.37 and acceptance ≤ 31% (`outputs/study3_v0.1/results.json`; 35 of 384 key-level × forgery-set cells at or above 50%).
- *Inference.* A defence evaluated only against this estimator would defend against an attack that already fails for the typical key; the result would be uninformative whichever way it came out.
- *Proposal.* Study 3 showed that the **gradient** statistic sees the key far better than activations (96–100% single-text detection at a 1% FPR; the raw activation cosine detected 0% in the power pilot). The attacker, who has the open model, can compute the same standardised gradients at every layer and **average them over the *n* observed texts** (call it Route A′). Nothing in Studies 1–3 tested it. If Route A′ recovers the fixed key at modest *n*, Study 5 has a real threat to defend against and the paper's attack result sharpens (the detector's best statistic is also the thief's). If it does not, the fixed key is already stealing-resistant at this budget and Study 5's question must change. This is the gate's single next check (§5).

## 2. The design space (analysis; no new evidence)
| Variant | The key depends on | The detector needs | The attacker's handle | Expected robustness cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed (*h* = 0, *K* = 1) | the secret | the text | average over outputs | none beyond Studies 2–3 | the comparator |
| (a) per-token context hashing, width *h* | the secret and the previous *h* tokens | the text (it re-derives the contexts) | group positions by their observable context; one estimate per frequent context | grows with *h*: a paraphrase keeps few *n*-grams, so the re-derived key is wrong wherever the context changed | the KGW and SynthID mechanism; the first *h* positions have no complete context and are neither steered nor scored |
| (b) rotation among *K* secret keys, one per text | the secret and a per-text draw | the text (it tests the union of the *K* keys, calibrated as a max statistic) | averaging gives the mean of the *K* keys (cosine about 1/√K with each); a clustering attacker can separate the groups; one recovered key suffices against a union test | none per text; a power cost from the union | the No Free Lunch and SynthID multi-key design; the "told which key" bound is Study 3's single-key result; **buildable from the existing 8-key corpus with little new generation** |
| (c) per-prompt key | the secret and a hash of the prompt | the text **and the prompt** | resample the same prompt *n* times | none if the prompt is supplied; total if not | metadata at detection (SLAM's document-id design); an attacker who controls prompts reduces it to the fixed case |
| (d) per-text key from the text's own opening | the secret and the first *h*₀ continuation tokens | the text | force or copy the opening | total if a paraphrase changes the opening | dominated by (a) and (b) |

*Inference:* (a) and (b) are the candidates worth testing; (c) and (d) are recorded as metadata-dependent or dominated. (a) at two widths plus (b) gives three points on the stealability–robustness frontier beside the fixed key: the frontier that No Free Lunch and the Reliability paper mapped for token schemes.

## 4. Occupied, residual gap, meaning, feasibility
**Occupied.** The ideas of context-hashed and rotating keys (KGW, SynthID, the cryptographic line); the finding that several keys lower stealing-based spoofing at a cost in removal robustness and detection power (No Free Lunch; Learnability); the finding that a larger context width resists stealing but lowers paraphrase robustness (Reliability; No Free Lunch; Watermark Stealing); per-document keyed activation steering with metadata (SLAM); the proposal of rotating vectors for Self-Recognition (its authors, as future work).

**Residual gap (search level).** No work implements or measures a context-keyed or rotating direction for dense activation steering. Nobody has measured, in the activation family, (i) the key-recovery cost against the number of contexts or keys, (ii) the paraphrase-robustness cost of context-dependent directions under an exact per-key-calibrated test, (iii) the quality cost at matched strength, or (iv) the detection-power cost of a union test. **Type:** an adequacy check of a transferred defence (an applied evaluation), not a new watermark.

**Is the gap meaningful?** Yes, conditionally. A deployer of Self-Recognition-style steering must choose between a fixed vector and a keyed one; the measurement says what each choice costs. Activation watermarks are motivated by paraphrase robustness (Self-Recognition; SLAM), and context keying may remove exactly that advantage, which would be a useful negative result: "the no-free-lunch trade-off carries over to the activation family". The condition: the measurement is meaningful only if there is an attack to defend against at the budget studied (§1).

**Feasibility (evidence from the locked runs).** Generation costs 1.07–1.66 s per 256-token text on Qwen2.5-1.5B (calibration and feasibility pilot); the gradient feature pass 0.21 s per text (Study 3 pilot; Study 3 re-scored about 53,000 texts in 2.9 h); P-Qwen paraphrase with the attacker's self-check 3.7 h per 3,200 originals (Study 2 phase 1). A keyed generation needs a stateful hook (the per-position key from the tokens so far); the per-position detector needs per-position gradients, which one backward pass gives. The rotation arm needs almost no new generation (the 8 study keys' texts already form a rotating-key corpus); the context-hashed arms need new observed sets. A design of 2 strengths × 8 keys × 2 hashed arms, with *n* ≤ 1,024 observed texts per key-level, is about 2 × 8 × 2 × 1,324 generations, about 20 h at 1.3 s, plus features and paraphrase. Scope therefore needs a timing check and probably *n* ≤ 256 for the hashed arms; a tuning-key pilot first, as for Study 2. Precision: keys are the replicates (8), 100 texts per key-level, as in Studies 2–3.

## 5. Verdict: CONDITIONAL
**The single next check: the threat.** A tuning-key pilot of Route A′, the gradient-averaging key-recovery attack on the **fixed** key, using the existing calibration texts (tuning keys 9001–9004 at ρ = 0.35, 0.50 and 0.70, 100 texts per key-level; the attacker's reference gradients from Study 1's pool C texts). The attacker computes the gradient of the log-likelihood with respect to an additive vector at every layer in one backward pass, standardises against its own reference, averages over the *n* observed texts, picks the layer by its own z-score profile and the support by the largest |z| (the same selection as Route A, with gradients in place of activations). **Pre-set reading:** the threat is real if the median cos(v̂, v) over the 4 tuning keys reaches 0.5 at some *n* ≤ 100 at ρ = 0.50 or 0.70; if it stays below 0.5 at *n* = 100, the pilot is extended to *n* = 1,024 (about 1.5 h of generation per level) before any verdict. Cost of the first stage: about 15 minutes of compute. Nothing in it touches a study key.

**If the threat is real:** Study 5 proceeds as the defence against Route A′ (with Route A kept for continuity), on candidates (a) and (b). **If it is not:** the fixed key is stealing-resistant at this budget against both estimators; Study 5 is re-planned (for example around the few leaking keys, or dropped in favour of Study 4 or the claim gate), and the paper's attack claim is narrowed accordingly.

**Wording constraints for the paper (whatever the outcome):**
- "a transfer of context hashing (KGW; Kirchenbauer et al. 2023) and key rotation (Pang et al. 2024; SynthID; Gu et al. 2024) to activation steering, named as future work by Ardoin et al. (2026)";
- "the first measurement, to our knowledge, of the stealability–robustness–quality trade-off for an activation-level scheme" (search level; absence is not proof);
- never "immune to stealing": the token-level literature shows a cost multiplier, and the attacker budget is bounded (*n* ≤ 1,024 here against 30,000 responses in Jovanović et al.);
- not a new watermark; not a fix of the trade-off (SEEK is a token-level fix).

**Scoop risk:** named by the Self-Recognition authors in June 2026; no follow-up visible after four months (Semantic Scholar, arXiv author search, the repository). Speed matters.

## 7. Condition resolved (2026-10-02)
The threat check ran on the tuning keys after Yichen's approval (`study5_pilot/THREAT_CHECK_SPEC_v0.1.md`, FIXED; results in `outputs/study5_threat_v0.1/`). **The threat is real:** the gradient-averaging attacker recovers the fixed key with a median cosine of 0.95 from 16 texts at ρ = 0.35 and 0.93 from 64 texts at ρ = 0.50 (0.57 from 100 texts at ρ = 0.70), where Study 1's activation estimator recovers nothing on the same texts. The gap is therefore meaningful: there is an attack to defend against, and it is cheap. **Verdict: PASS as a transfer**, with the wording constraints of §5 unchanged and one addition: the paper's attack claim is strengthened to "the exact test's statistic averaged over tens of outputs recovers the fixed key" once Study 5's fixed-key arm confirms it on the study keys.
