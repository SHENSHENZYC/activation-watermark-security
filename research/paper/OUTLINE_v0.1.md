## 2. One-paragraph pitch (the abstract's seed; obeys rules 1, 2, 4, 5, 7)
Activation-steering watermarks add a secret vector to a hidden layer during generation and detect it from the model's internal activations when the text is re-read, which their authors motivate by robustness to paraphrase. We present, to our knowledge, the first security analysis of such a watermark: Self-Recognition's construction (Ardoin et al., ICML 2026), re-implemented on Qwen2.5-1.5B at 256 tokens, attacked by a passive adversary who holds the open weights and up to 1,024 watermarked outputs but no detector access, under pre-registered protocols. First, under our measures the published strength did not reproduce on 1–3B base models: no strength made the scheme's trained probe reliably detectable without a perplexity cost. Second, that probe (re-trained by us) accepts steering that is not the key, so an attacker without the key produces fluent forgeries it accepts at the one strength where quality is acceptable. Third, an exact key-resampling score test — a transfer of GaussMark's statistic to an additive activation vector — detects 96–100% of genuine texts at an exact 1% false-positive rate (exact on average over keys; per-key rates checked) and rejects those forgeries. Fourth, the same statistic, averaged over 64 observed outputs, recovers the fixed key (median cosine 0.94–0.95) and its forgeries then pass as often as genuine text; an activation-mean estimator recovers nothing from 1,024. Fifth, an off-the-shelf paraphrase scrubs the test's evidence at the quality-acceptable strengths and not above them. Sixth, keying the direction by the previous h tokens or rotating among eight keys — transfers of context hashing and key rotation from token-level watermarking, named as future work by the scheme's authors — blocks this attacker at n ≤ 1,024 and improves perplexity, but context keying loses most of the paraphrase robustness and rotation falls to a clustering attacker from 64–256 texts. Across the studies, for the fixed key on this model, the strengths that are detectable and paraphrase-robust carry a quality cost, the quality-neutral strengths are scrubbed, and the published detector type is forgeable at either. We release the protocols, locks and code.

## 3. Contributions (copied from the gate's wording verdicts)
**Claimed (as narrowed; bounds: Qwen2.5-1.5B base, layer 14, keys 1001–1008, ρ ∈ {0.25, 0.35, 0.50, 0.70}, 256 tokens, Routes A/A′/B with n ≤ 1,024, P-Qwen and P-Phi, medians over 8 keys with cluster-bootstrap CIs):**
1. **C1 (headline, narrowed):** to our knowledge, the first security analysis of an activation-steering LLM watermark — key recovery, forgery, scrubbing, an exact test and a keyed defence — on one published scheme, re-implemented; SLAM and AWM are discussed, not tested.
2. **C2 (narrowed):** the scheme's published detector type, a trained per-token MLP probe re-implemented and re-trained by us, accepts steering that is not the key (Route B's vectors at median cosine ≤ 0.062) and garbled text; practical fluent forgery without the key is claimed **only at ρ = 0.35** (v0.4 F1); 0.50 and 0.70 are inconclusive; the authors' own paraphrase-spoofing observation is cited (rule 7).
3. **C3 (narrowed):** an exact key-resampling score test — **a transfer of GaussMark's statistic** with resampled sparse keys — detects 96.0–100.0% at an exact 1% FPR, beats the probe at ρ ≤ 0.50, has a 0.68% FPR on human text, attributes random-key texts 0.0%, and makes Study 1's forgeries not practical for the median key; **exact on average over keys** (rule 3).
4. **C4 (claimed, with bounds):** the first measured key-recovery attack on an activation-steering watermark (search level): the detector's statistic averaged over 64 outputs recovers the fixed key (cosine 0.94–0.95) and its forgeries pass as often as genuine text; the activation-mean estimator fails at 1,024. "The detector's best statistic is also the thief's best estimator" is a measured fact with an inferential explanation. Budgets are compared with token-level stealing **with the access-model caveat** (rule 4).
5. **C5 (claimed, with bounds):** the first scrubbing evaluation of an activation-steering watermark under an exact, per-key-calibrated test at matched quality, with an insider (true-key) bound; the comparison with the authors' robustness figures is indirect.
6. **C6 (narrowed; labelled interpretation):** the measured trade-off curve along ρ for this family on this model; "not the first to note a trade-off" (rule 10).
7. **C7 (narrowed to a transfer measurement; decision 3):** context hashing (h = 1, 4) and rotation among 8 keys, transfers from Kirchenbauer et al. (2023a, 2024), Pang et al. (2024), Gu et al. (2024) and SynthID-Text, named as future work by Ardoin et al. (2026): stealing blocked at n ≤ 1,024 **by this attacker**, perplexity improved, paraphrase robustness lost (material for h = 1 at both strengths and h = 4 at 0.50; **inconclusive** for h = 4 at 0.35); rotation falls to clustering at ρ = 0.50 (pre-registered) and at 0.35 **only through the labelled addendum** (rule 6).
8. **C8 (narrowed; decision 2):** under our conditions the published setting did not reproduce on 1–3B base models; the detectable-and-fluent window exists for the exact test (ρ = 0.25) and not for the probe; their Table 7 beside ours; never "their claims are false".
9. **C9 (claimed as a practical finding):** key-resampling p-values are exact on average over keys; a deployer with one key must check that key's own false-positive rate on (paraphrased) null text; a known property, stated for GaussMark by its authors.
10. **C13 (methodological, "as others have noted"):** a forgery or scrubbing quality condition must pair perplexity with a repetition term and be read against reference samples.

**Labelled leads, inferences and descriptives:** C10 per-key heterogeneity (descriptive; per-key tables beside every median); C11 the probe accepts 79–100% of paraphrases at ρ ≥ 0.50 (lead only); C12 the probe detects off-distribution activations more than the key (inference; "consistent with").

**Not claimed (from the brief and every protocol):** the first stealing attack on LLM watermarks; a new watermark; immunity to stealing; a fix of the trade-off; the paraphrase-robustness limit; results on other models, layers, text lengths, key distributions or at 8B scale; SLAM-style or AWM-style designs; adaptive attackers who know the exact test; human-judged quality; budgets above 1,024 texts; Pang et al.'s repeated-query removal attack against multiple keys.

## 7. The regulator framing paragraph (rule 12; for §1, after the motivation)
> Regulators now name the properties this paper measures. The European Commission's Code of Practice on marking and labelling of AI-generated content (final, 10 June 2026) expects an imperceptible watermark on free-form text longer than 200 tokens (Sub-measure 1.1.2), measures *reliability* by the error rate of detection on the provider's own marked content and on other content (Measure 3.2), and asks that solutions keep their performance "under varying conditions, covering both common alterations and adversarial attacks", paraphrasing among them (Measure 3.3). NIST AI 100-4 defines a *secure* watermark as one that resists attempts "to remove or tamper with the watermark information, or to insert forged watermarks" (p. 13) and lists removal and forgery attempts among what an evaluation should cover (p. 38). A false-positive rate at a fixed level, detection after paraphrase, and resistance to forged watermarks are exactly the three measurements below. We use these texts for vocabulary and motivation only; nothing in this paper is a statement about any provider's compliance or deployment.
