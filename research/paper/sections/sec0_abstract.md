# Abstract

Activation-steering watermarks add a secret vector to a hidden layer during generation and detect it from the model's internal activations when the text is re-read; their authors motivate the design by robustness to paraphrase. We present, to our knowledge, the first security analysis of such a watermark: the construction of LLM Self-Recognition [@ardoin2026selfrecognition], re-implemented on Qwen2.5-1.5B at [[new_tokens]] tokens and measured under [[t1_n_studies]] pre-registered protocols against a passive attacker who holds the open weights and up to [[s5x_N_OBS]] watermarked outputs but cannot query the detector. First, under our measures the published strength did not reproduce on 1–3B base models: no strength made the scheme's trained probe reliably detectable without a perplexity cost. Second, that probe, re-trained by us, accepts steering that is not the key, so an attacker without the key produces fluent forgeries it accepts at the one strength where quality is acceptable ([[t3_v04_B_n256_FA_med_035]]% against a bar of [[t3_v04_bar_035]]%). Third, an exact key-resampling score test, a transfer of GaussMark's statistic to an additive activation vector, attributes [[s3x_oracle_tpr_min]]–[[s3x_oracle_tpr_max]]% of genuine texts at an exact [[fpr_nominal_pct]]% false-positive rate (exact on average over keys; every key's rate checked) and rejects those forgeries. Fourth, the same statistic, averaged over 64 observed outputs, recovers the fixed key at the known layer (median cosine [[s5_fixed_035_n64_cos_known]] and [[s5_fixed_05_n64_cos_known]]) and finds the layer for most keys when it searches, and its forgeries are attributed as often as genuine text ([[s5x_fixed_035_n64_FA_med]] and [[s5x_fixed_05_n64_FA_med]]% against [[s5x_fixed_035_oracle_FA_med]] and [[s5x_fixed_05_oracle_FA_med]]% at the known layer; [[s5_fixed_035_forge_search_FA_med]] and [[s5_fixed_05_forge_search_FA_med]]% with the layer search from [[s5_fixed_035_forge_search_n]] texts); an activation-mean estimator recovers nothing from [[s5x_N_OBS]]. Fifth, an off-the-shelf paraphrase scrubs the test's evidence at the quality-acceptable strengths ([[s2_S1_qwen_025_success_med]] and [[s2_S1_qwen_035_success_med]]% success) and not above them. Sixth, keying the direction by the previous $h$ tokens or rotating among [[n_keys]] keys, transfers of context hashing and key rotation from token-level watermarking that the scheme's authors name as future work, blocks this attacker at $n \leq$ [[s5x_N_OBS]]: the context-keyed arms also improve perplexity but lose most of the paraphrase robustness, and rotation, whose quality is unchanged, falls to a clustering attacker from 64 texts at $\rho = 0.50$ (and, post hoc, at 0.35). For the fixed key on this model, the strengths that are detectable and paraphrase-robust carry a quality cost (perplexity ratios [[cal_q15_05_ratio]] and [[cal_q15_07_ratio]]), the quality-neutral strengths are scrubbed, and the published detector type is forgeable without the key at the quality-neutral strength (and, by plain acceptance, above it). The protocols, locks and code accompany the paper.
