## 5.5 Scrubbing under the exact test

Study 2 scrubs the fixed key's genuine texts and scores the result with the exact test at matched quality (Section 4; Table T5; Figure F5): [[s5x_N_PARA]] genuine texts per key and strength, paraphrased by P-Qwen and P-Phi with the attacker's self-check and up to three attempts, or edited at 5% of the words by the true key (an insider bound), by the attacker's Route A estimate from Study 1, or at random. The test stayed calibrated on paraphrased null text (pooled false-positive rates [[s2_G1_qwen_pct]]% and [[s2_G1_phi_pct]]%; every key at [[s2_G2_qwen_range]]% and [[s2_G2_phi_range]]%) and attributed the unscrubbed originals at [[s2_P1_min]]–[[s2_P1_max]]%.

**Paraphrase scrubs at the quality-acceptable strengths and not above them.** At $\rho = 0.25$ both paraphrasers are **effective**: success [[s2_S1_qwen_025_success]]% for P-Qwen and [[s2_S1_phi_025_success]]% for P-Phi against the originals' miss rates of [[s2_S1_qwen_025_miss]]% and [[s2_S1_phi_025_miss]]%, with [[s2_S1_qwen_025_keys]] and [[s2_S1_phi_025_keys]] of [[n_keys]] keys at or above 50%. At $\rho = 0.35$ P-Qwen is **effective** ([[s2_S1_qwen_035_success]]%; [[s2_S1_qwen_035_keys]] of [[n_keys]] keys) and P-Phi **inconclusive** ([[s2_S1_phi_035_success]]%). At $\rho = 0.50$ and 0.70 **neither is effective** (P-Qwen [[s2_S1_qwen_05_success_med]] and [[s2_S1_qwen_07_success_med]]%; P-Phi [[s2_S1_phi_05_success_med]] and [[s2_S1_phi_07_success_med]]%; one key in each cell reaches 50%). The evidence is removed, not shortened: the originals cut to the paraphrase's length are still attributed [[s2_lm_min]]–[[s2_lm_max]]% of the time. At high strength quality is the other half of success: the share of P-Qwen's paraphrases passing all four conditions falls to [[s2_S1_qwen_07_pass_all]]% at $\rho = 0.70$.

**The true key helps only where the watermark is weak.** Edits of 5% of the words guided by the true key are **effective only at $\rho = 0.25$** (success [[s2_Etrue_025_success]]%; all [[n_keys]] keys), **inconclusive at 0.35** ([[s2_Etrue_035_success]]%) and not effective above (at 10% of the words the key removes the evidence at a visible fluency cost; App. F). Edits guided by the attacker's Route A estimate are **never effective** (success [[s2_Eest_success_max]]% at $\rho = 0.25$, falling to [[s2_Eest_success_min]]%), although they beat random edits at $\rho = 0.25$ and 0.35 (C1: [[s2_C1_025]] and [[s2_C1_035]] points; the estimate helps) and not above.

Two secondary readings. The per-key false-positive rate disperses after paraphrase: over 1,000 fresh keys of the public distribution the mean stays at [[s2_fresh_qwen_mean_pct]]% and [[s2_fresh_phi_mean_pct]]% (exact on average), but [[s2_fresh_qwen_share_above3_pct]]% and [[s2_fresh_phi_share_above3_pct]]% of keys exceed 3% (maxima [[s2_fresh_qwen_max_pct]]% and [[s2_fresh_phi_max_pct]]%), so a deployer should check their own key on paraphrased null text, the practical form of Section 3's "on average over keys" caveat. And the owner's trained probe accepts most of the same paraphrases at $\rho \geq 0.50$ where the exact test does not, which we record as a lead, not a result (App. F). The comparison with the authors' robustness figures is indirect: their detector, models, paraphraser and metric differ, and their Table 3 reports attribution accuracy between two steered variants rather than detection against human text at a fixed false-positive rate [@ardoin2026selfrecognition].
