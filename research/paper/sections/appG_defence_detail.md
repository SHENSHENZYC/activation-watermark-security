## G.2 The rotation addendum (post hoc, labelled)

**Table TA13b** (see `build/TA13b.md`).

Study 5's pre-registered rotation attacker clustered the standardised summed gradients of the observed texts into $K = 8$ groups, estimated a direction per cluster and forged with the cluster whose standardised mean-gradient profile was largest. At $\rho = 0.50$ that rule picked a cluster aligned with a key and the forgery was practical from $n = 64$ (Section 5.6). At $\rho = 0.35$ the recovery succeeded ([[s5x_rot_035_n256_keys_recovered]] of [[n_keys]] keys at cosine $\geq 0.5$ from $n = 256$) but the rule picked a small cluster whose estimate has cosine [[ta13_add_n256_study_cos]] with every key (its profile, [[ta13_add_n256_study_profile]], is an outlier of [[ta13_add_n256_study_size]] texts), so the pre-registered verdict read "stealing blocked" where the key had been recovered. After the run, a fixed specification (written and hashed before it was executed, not pre-registered with the study) forged with every cluster's estimate at $\rho = 0.35$, 100 texts each, tested by the union test under the level's pooled fluency bars (perplexity $\leq$ [[ta13_add_cut_ppl]], seq-rep-4 $\leq$ [[ta13_add_cut_rep]]). Table TA13b lists every cluster. At $n = 256$ the study's rule gives [[ta13_add_n256_study_FA]]%, the largest cluster [[ta13_add_n256_largest_FA]]%, and an attacker who tries every cluster reaches [[ta13_add_n256_every_FA]]% (cosine [[ta13_add_n256_every_cos]]); [[ta13_add_n256_reaching_bar]] of the [[ta13_add_n256_clusters_ge05]] clusters with cosine $\geq 0.5$ reach the D1 bar of [[ta13_add_bar]]%. At $n =$ [[s5x_N_OBS]] the three readings are [[ta13_add_n1024_study_FA]]%, [[ta13_add_n1024_largest_FA]]% and [[ta13_add_n1024_every_FA]]%, with [[ta13_add_n1024_reaching_bar]] of [[ta13_add_n1024_clusters_ge05]] clusters reaching the bar. Study 5's D1 verdict for rotation at $\rho = 0.35$ stands as pre-registered; the paper states the strength's result only through this labelled addendum beside it (Section 5.6, Table T6, Figure F6). The methodological lesson is recorded for future protocols: pre-register the recovery metric and the forgery as separate readings, and let the attacker forge with every candidate or with its own best-by-objective candidate, never with a single heuristic pick.

## G.4 What the per-context attacker gets from $n$ texts

Figure FA4 (Appendix C) shows the mechanics behind D1. For $h = 1$ the attacker covers [[fa4_h1_035_cov_n1024]] of a genuine text's positions with an estimate at $n =$ [[s5x_N_OBS]] (from [[fa4_h1_035_cov_n64]] at $n = 64$), having estimated [[fa4_h1_035_ctx_n1024]] contexts, and its estimates improve with how often a context was seen: contexts seen 512 times or more reach a cosine of [[fa4_h1_05_cosbin_512]] with their true direction at $\rho = 0.50$, against [[fa4_h1_05_cosbin_16]] for those seen 16–31 times. The rising acceptance of its forgeries with $n$ (Section 5.6) is this curve, and it is why "blocked at $n \leq$ [[s5x_N_OBS]]" is a statement about the budget. For $h = 4$ almost no context recurs 16 times among [[s5x_N_OBS]] texts (coverage [[fa4_h4_035_cov_n1024]]; [[fa4_h4_035_ctx_n1024]] contexts), so that arm is starved of data rather than resistant in principle; its cost is the robustness lost (G.1).
