## 5.6 Keyed directions: context hashing and rotation, as transfers

Study 5 transfers two token-level defences to activation steering: keying the direction by the previous $h$ tokens, the context hashing of Kirchenbauer et al. [@kirchenbauer2023watermark; @kirchenbauer2024reliability], and rotating among $K$ keys with a union test, the multiple-key design of the same authors [@kirchenbauer2023watermark] as studied by Pang et al. and Gu et al. [@pang2024nofreelunch; @gu2024learnability] and deployed by SynthID-Text [@dathathri2024synthid]; both were named as future work by the scheme's authors (p. 7) [@ardoin2026selfrecognition]. The arms are $h = 1$, $h = 4$ and rotation among $K = 8$ keys, beside the fixed key, at $\rho = 0.35$ and 0.50 with the same [[n_keys]] keys, against the attacker of Section 5.4 adapted to each arm (per observable context for the hashed arms; on the whole mixture, the naive attacker, or per cluster, the clustering attacker, for rotation; App. C) at $n \in \{64, 256, \text{[[s5x_N_OBS]]}\}$, with Study 2's paraphrase pipeline for the robustness cost. The gates passed (pooled false-positive rates [[s5x_h1_G1_pct]], [[s5x_h4_G1_pct]] and [[s5x_union_G1_pct]]%; every key calibrated) and the keyed tests attributed [[s5x_arms_P1_min]]–[[s5x_arms_P1_max]]% of their genuine texts, the union test [[s5x_rot_035_union_detect_med]] and [[s5x_rot_05_union_detect_med]]% (P1; Table T6a).

**Stealing (D1).** Both hashed arms **block this attacker at $n \leq \text{[[s5x_N_OBS]]}$**: the per-context attacker's forgeries are attributed at most [[s5x_arms_FA_med_max]]% (medians; upper bounds at most [[s5x_arms_FA_hi_max]]%) against bars of 40–44%, half the arms' genuine FA (Table T6a; Figure F4); its per-context estimates stay poor (count-weighted cosine [[s5_h1_05_n1024_cosw]] for $h = 1$ at $n = \text{[[s5x_N_OBS]]}$, $\rho = 0.50$; App. C). The verdict is bounded by the budget, not by the scheme: $h = 1$'s acceptance rises with $n$ at $\rho = 0.50$ ([[s5x_h1_05_FA_trend]]%), and the token-level literature steals context-hashed schemes with tens of thousands of responses (Section 2.2). Rotation blocks the naive attacker at both strengths but **falls to the clustering attacker at $\rho = 0.50$, practical at $n = 64$**: it separates the mixture into the keys ([[s5_rot_05_n64_keys_recovered]] of [[n_keys]] recovered at cosine $\geq 0.5$ from 64 texts, [[s5_rot_05_n256_keys_recovered]] of [[n_keys]] from 256) and the union test attributes [[s5x_rot_05_n64_cluster_FA_med]]–[[s5x_rot_05_n1024_cluster_FA_med]]% of its forgeries. At $\rho = 0.35$ the pre-registered verdict is **stealing blocked at $n \leq \text{[[s5x_N_OBS]]}$**, and it stands as written; its reason is the attacker's pre-set rule for choosing the forging cluster, which at $n = 256$ and [[s5x_N_OBS]] picked an outlier cluster with cosine [[s5_rot_035_n256_chosen_cos]] to every key while the other clusters recovered [[s5_rot_035_n256_keys_recovered]] of [[n_keys]]. A post-hoc addendum, labelled and not pre-registered (App. G; the last rows of Table T6a; the hollow marker in Figure F6), forged with every cluster in turn: the best cluster's forgeries are attributed [[s5x_add_n256_every_FA]]% at $n = 256$ and [[s5x_add_n1024_every_FA]]% at $n = \text{[[s5x_N_OBS]]}$, and [[s5x_add_n1024_reaching_bar]] of the [[s5x_add_n1024_clusters_ge05]] recovered clusters reach the bar. Read with the pre-registered result, rotation among [[n_keys]] keys multiplies this attacker's cost by about $K$ against averaging and by nothing against clustering, at both strengths.

**Robustness (D2).** The hashed arms lose most of the paraphrase robustness the fixed key has (Table T6b; Figure F6). After P-Qwen with the attacker's self-check, scrub success is [[s5x_h1_035_para_success_med]] and [[s5x_h1_05_para_success_med]]% against $h = 1$ and [[s5x_h4_035_para_success_med]] and [[s5x_h4_05_para_success_med]]% against $h = 4$, where the fixed key on the same prompts and keys was scrubbed [[s2_S1_qwen_035_success_med]] and [[s2_S1_qwen_05_success_med]]% of the time (Section 5.5). The paired differences are **material** for $h = 1$ at both strengths ([[s5x_h1_035_d]] and [[s5x_h1_05_d]] points) and for $h = 4$ at $\rho = 0.50$ ([[s5x_h4_05_d]]), and **inconclusive** for $h = 4$ at $\rho = 0.35$ ([[s5x_h4_035_d]]: the median falls under the 20-point bar although the interval excludes zero). Attribution after paraphrase falls to [[s5x_arms_para_detected_min]]–[[s5x_arms_para_detected_max]]% for the keyed arms against the fixed key's [[s2_S1_qwen_035_detected_after]] and [[s2_S1_qwen_05_detected_after]]%: a paraphrase changes most contexts, so the content shift that survives it no longer lines up with any per-context key the owner can test (an inference; the same mechanism denies the attacker a stable direction to average). Rotation keeps the fixed key's robustness: the union test's loss on Study 2's paraphrases is [[s5x_rot_035_d]] and [[s5x_rot_05_d]] points, **immaterial** by the 20-point bar.

**Quality (D3).** Context keying costs nothing by our proxy and improves it, with perplexity ratios to the fixed key's texts on the same prompts of [[s5x_ppl_ratio_min]]–[[s5x_ppl_ratio_max]] (medians; no key above the 1.10 bar; **immaterial** everywhere), which is an improvement by that measure and not a quality claim (Section 4); rotation's quality is unchanged by construction.

**The point of difference with the token-level evidence.** That evidence on multiple keys is mixed (Section 6); our rotation result sits with Jovanović et al.'s [@jovanovic2024stealing] under this access model rather than with Pang et al.'s [@pang2024nofreelunch], whose removal attack is untested here, so the no-free-lunch trade-off transfers only in this form: the context-width trade-off between stealing and paraphrase robustness reappears, rotation buys a constant factor against averaging and nothing against clustering, and immunity is claimed for no arm.
