# Appendix B — The exact test in detail

## B.1 Features and passes

For a text, the detector takes its first [[new_tokens]] continuation tokens, without the prompt, and runs one forward and one backward pass at batch size 1 with an additive vector $b$ registered at the output of decoder layer [[layer]] (a forward hook adding $b$ to the hidden state; the model's parameters do not require gradients). The loss is the summed log-likelihood of the continuation tokens; $g = \partial \log p(y) / \partial b$ at $b = 0$ is a vector in $\mathbb{R}^{[[d_model]]}$. For the per-position variant, $b$ has one row per position and the backward pass returns $g_t$ for every content position at once. The pilot's validation checked the gradient against finite differences along eight random directions on an unwatermarked text (correlation [[pilot_grad_fd_r]]) and that the hook leaves the model's logits unchanged after removal; p-values on synthetic null data were uniform (Kolmogorov–Smirnov $p$ = [[pilot_ks_p]]) and lay on the $1/1{,}000$ grid. One forward-and-backward pass takes about [[pilot_s_per_text]] s per text on the laptop.

## B.2 The four candidate statistics and the power pilot

The pilot fixed four candidates, each turning a text into a vector $x$ with $T(x, v) = \langle x, v \rangle / (\lVert x \rVert \lVert v \rVert)$: **S1**, the mean over tokens of the unit-normalised activation at layer [[layer]] (the quantity behind the scheme's training-free cosine statistic); **S2**, S1 centred and scaled per coordinate against the owner's [[pool_F]] reference texts; **S3**, the gradient $g$; **S4**, $g$ centred and scaled per coordinate. The exact p-value, the $M = [[pilot_M]]$ public null keys and the [[pilot_alpha_pct]]% level are those of Section 3. The pilot ran on [[pilot_n_tuning_keys]] tuning keys with 100 watermarked texts per key and strength (from the calibration runs) and 400 unwatermarked texts.

**The pre-set rule and its outcome.** The rule scored each candidate by the mean over the three working strengths of its median detection rate, chose the top score, with ties within 2 points broken in the fixed order S3, S4, S2, S1, and required an **implementation check**: each candidate's pooled false-positive rate on the 400 unwatermarked texts against all four keys ([[pilot_n_fpr_tests]] tests) within [0.3, 2.5]%, or no candidate is chosen. S1's rate was [[pilot_S1_fpr_pct]]% (fail), S2's [[pilot_S2_fpr_pct]]%, S3's [[pilot_S3_fpr_pct]]% and S4's [[pilot_S4_fpr_pct]]% (pass); the scores were [[pilot_S1_score]], [[pilot_S2_score]], [[pilot_S3_score]] and [[pilot_S4_score]], so [[pilot_tie_candidates]] tied at the top ([[pilot_top_score]]). **By the rule, no primary was chosen**, because the check's wording let one candidate's failure block the choice among the others. We state this plainly: the check was written to catch code bugs, and it did something else.

**The exploratory diagnosis (written and run after the pilot's outcomes were read).** S1's failure is not a feature bug: on the calibration's own test split S1 reproduces the calibration's cosine AUROC exactly. It is a property of the statistic under key resampling. The per-coordinate scale of the raw activation mean varies [[pilot_spread_act]]-fold across coordinates (against [[pilot_spread_grad]]-fold for the gradient), so the activation mean is nearly the same vector for every text and a given key's rank among the null keys is almost fixed: a key either never fires or always fires. Over [[pilot_posthoc_n_keys]] fresh random keys, each tested on the [[pilot_posthoc_n_texts]] unwatermarked texts, S1's mean false-positive rate was [[pilot_S1_perkey_mean_pct]]% (exact on average, as the theory says) while [[pilot_S1_perkey_share_zero_pct]]% of keys never fired and [[pilot_S1_perkey_share_gt50_pct]]% fired on more than half of the texts. The other candidates' per-key rates: S2 mean [[pilot_S2_perkey_mean_pct]]%, maximum [[pilot_S2_perkey_max_pct]]%; S3 mean [[pilot_S3_perkey_mean_pct]]%, maximum [[pilot_S3_perkey_max_pct]]%, [[pilot_S3_perkey_share_gt5_pct]]% of keys above 5%; S4 mean [[pilot_S4_perkey_mean_pct]]%, maximum [[pilot_S4_perkey_max_pct]]%, [[pilot_S4_perkey_share_gt5_pct]]% above 5%. Centring and scaling each coordinate is what keeps every key near the nominal level.

**The choice.** S4 was chosen as the primary statistic by decision, after the diagnosis and before Study 3 was designed, for its per-key calibration; S3 is Study 3's secondary. Table TA2 gives the pilot in full: S3 and S4 detected [[pilot_S3_tpr_working_min]]–[[pilot_S3_tpr_working_max]]% and [[pilot_S4_tpr_working_min]]–[[pilot_S4_tpr_working_max]]% of single texts at the working strengths and [[pilot_S3_tpr_025]]% and [[pilot_S4_tpr_025]]% at $\rho = 0.25$; S1 detected [[pilot_S1_tpr_working_max]]% and S2 [[pilot_S2_tpr_working_min]]–[[pilot_S2_tpr_working_max]]%; texts steered by another tuning key were detected at [[pilot_S4_crosskey_min]]–[[pilot_S4_crosskey_max]]% by S4 ([[pilot_n_crosskey_tests]] tests per cell; about 1% is expected by construction), so the gradient tests detect the key, not steering in general. ## B.4 The union test and the keyed per-position test

**Rotation.** The owner holds $K$ keys and does not know which one a text used. With $x$ the standardised gradient of Section 3, the statistic is $U(x) = \max_{k \leq K} \langle x, \hat v_k \rangle$ over the unit key directions. Its null distribution is obtained by resampling whole key sets: the 9,999 public null keys are cut into 999 disjoint groups of $K$, and $p = (1 + \#\{j : U_j \geq U\}) / 1{,}000$ with $U_j$ the maximum over group $j$. Under the null the owner's key set and the null groups are exchangeable, so the level is exact on average over key sets, as for one key. The design is that of Kirchenbauer et al. [@kirchenbauer2023watermark, p. 7], with the multiple-testing correction replaced by resampling the maximum; its power loss relative to the single-key test is measured in Section 5.6.

**Context-keyed directions.** The direction at continuation position $t \geq h$ is $v(c_t)$, a $k$-sparse unit vector derived by a keyed pseudorandom function from the secret and the previous $h$ token ids $c_t = (y_{t-h}, \dots, y_{t-1})$, scaled to $\rho N$; positions $t < h$ and the prompt are not steered. The function mixes the secret and the context with splitmix64 finalisers [@steele2014splitmix] and draws $k$ distinct coordinates and uniform values from the mixed state, a deterministic keyed mixing function, so that the party with the secret reproduces every position's key; no cryptographic property is claimed for it. The owner computes the per-position gradients $g_t$ in one backward pass, standardises them per coordinate with pooled position statistics from pool F, and forms $S(x, m) = \sum_{t \geq h} \langle x_t, v_m(c_t) \rangle$ for its secret $m$ and for 999 public null secrets; $p$ is the rank of the true secret's statistic. With $h = 0$ the statistic reduces to the summed-gradient form of S4 up to the standardisation, which the Study 5 validation checked against S4 on the tuning keys. The owner needs the text only; the detector reads the contexts from the text it is given, so the keyed test is a plain function of the text and the secret, as the fixed-key test is.

The per-position form with $h = 0$ was checked against S4 on the tuning keys in the defence pilot's I4 addendum (Appendix G, Table TA13c): [[ta13_i4_h0_035]]% against [[ta13_i4_S4_035]]% at $\rho = 0.35$ and [[ta13_i4_h0_05]]% against [[ta13_i4_S4_05]]% at $0.50$, within the 10 points the design required; it was not run on the study keys.

## B.5 The first-order sketch (a heuristic, not a derivation)

Let $p_b(y)$ be the model's distribution over continuations when $b$ is added at the steered layer at every position, and $s(y) = \nabla_b \log p_b(y) |_{b = 0}$ the score. For text drawn from $p_{\beta v}$ with a small $\beta$, a first-order expansion of the score's expectation gives $\mathbb{E}_{p_{\beta v}}[s(y)] \approx \beta F v$, with $F = \mathbb{E}_{p_0}[s s^\top]$ the Fisher information of $b$ at 0, because $\mathbb{E}_{p_0}[s] = 0$. Two readings follow. For the owner, $\langle v, s(y) \rangle$ is the score statistic for the alternative $b = \beta v$ against $b = 0$ [@rao1948score], which is why the gradient test has power; the resampling calibration replaces the asymptotic normal law, which does not hold for sparse keys. For the attacker, the sample mean of $s$ over $n$ observed outputs estimates $\beta F v$, so a sparse estimate of the mean after per-coordinate standardisation (an approximate whitening when $F$ is close to diagonal in the standardised coordinates) points along $v$. The assumptions are strong: the steering strength $\beta = \rho N$ is not small relative to the activation norms at the higher strengths, the [[new_tokens]]-token sum mixes positions, and $F$ is not diagonal. We therefore present the recovery result (Section 5.4) as a measurement and this sketch as the reason we expected it, not as a proof. Nothing in the paper depends on the sketch.
