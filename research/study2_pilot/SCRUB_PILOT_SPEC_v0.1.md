## 2. Methods (as Study 2 will run them)
### 2.1 Paraphrase (P-Qwen, P-Phi)
- **Input:** the continuation only. The attacker holds the output text; the prompt is not paraphrased.
- **Chat prompt P1** (user turn, model's own chat template): "Paraphrase the following text. Keep all of its information and meaning, but change the wording and sentence structure. Keep it about the same length. Reply with the paraphrase only.\n\nText:\n{text}"
- **Sampling:** temperature 0.7, top-p 0.9, at most 400 new tokens (Stage 4's cap), seed fixed per text and attempt. Batching: §2.4.
- **The attacker's self-check (up to 3 attempts).** An attempt is kept if it passes the attacker's own version of Study 2's quality conditions (§3), computed with the attacker's data instead of pool A:
  - length ≥ 80% of the original's tokens;
  - perplexity (Qwen2.5-1.5B, given the prompt) ≤ max(1.25 × the original's, the 95th percentile over the human continuations of its 200 pool C prompts);
  - seq-rep-4 ≤ max(the original's, the same human continuations' 95th percentile);
  - embedding cosine to the original > the 95th percentile of its 200 pool C pairs (reference text vs the human continuation of the same prompt).

  Texts whose attempt fails are paraphrased again with a new seed (attempts 2 and 3, in batches of the failing texts). If no attempt passes, the attacker keeps the attempt that fails the fewest conditions (ties: the higher cosine, then the earlier attempt). The attacker never sees a detector. A rational attacker retries a visibly bad paraphrase; single-attempt results are also reported (§4).

### 2.2 Key-guided edits (E-true, E-est) and random edits (E-rand)
**Principle.** S4 tests ⟨x, v⟩ with x = (g − μ)/σ, where g is the gradient of the text's log-likelihood with respect to an additive vector at layer 14. For a direction w, ⟨g, w⟩ is the derivative of the log-likelihood along w, a sum of one term per token. Each token's term, and the term every alternative token would have, can be read from two forward passes, so an editor can find the tokens that carry the key's signal and swap them for plausible tokens that do not.
- **Text:** tokens x₁ … x_T, the continuation's first 256 Qwen tokens, no prompt (exactly what the detector scores).
- **Contributions:** for a direction w at layer l, c_t(y) = [log p₊(y | x_<t) − log p₋(y | x_<t)] / 2s, where p₊ and p₋ are the next-token distributions with +s·ŵ and −s·ŵ added at the output of decoder layer l at every position (ŵ = w/‖w‖; s = 2.0, about 3.5% of the median activation norm at layer 14). The output layer is applied in float32, because per-token differences are small next to bfloat16 rounding of the logits. Σ_t c_t(x_t), over the positions the detector scores (t ≥ 2), is the central finite difference of ⟨g, ŵ⟩ (check I1).
- **Direction:** w = v ⊘ σ̂ (elementwise), where σ̂ is the per-coordinate standard deviation of g at layer l over the attacker's 200 pool C references. **E-true:** v = the true key, l = 14 (an insider who has the key but not the owner's reference texts). **E-est:** v and l = the attacker's estimate (in Study 2: Study 1 v0.2's Route A estimate at n = 1,024, from `K_*_summary.json` and `K_*_vhats.npy`; in this pilot, the stand-in below).
- **One round:** two forward passes over the current tokens, with +s·ŵ and with −s·ŵ, and full-vocabulary log-probabilities. The contributions are their central differences; the unsteered distribution, used for candidates and plausibility, is approximated by the midpoint of the two log-probabilities (error of order s²; this saves a third pass). An editable position t (t ≥ 2) holds a **whole word**: an alphabetic token with a leading space (for example 'Ġcat') followed by a word boundary (the next token starts with a space or a newline, or with a character that is not alphanumeric). Candidates are whole words among the 40 most likely tokens at t, at least **1/3 as likely as the current word**, other than the current word; the first 10 such are kept. gain_t = c_t(x_t) − min_y c_t(y), with y* the minimiser. The editor chooses the *r* positions with the largest positive gain among positions not yet edited and at least 2 apart from each other and from earlier edits, and replaces x_t by y*. Round size *r* = 2% of T (rounded up), subject to rule A2 (§6); a round never overshoots the next budget.
- **Why whole words and a plausibility limit (code tests on unwatermarked texts with a random direction, 2026-09-30):** without them, edits split words (for example 'groupschemistry'). Even with them, perplexity rises steeply with the number of edits: about ×1.4 at 2%, ×1.9 at 5%, ×3 at 10% and ×7.5 at 20% of tokens, for random edits too. Only small budgets are likely to pass the fluency condition, which is why the pilot also saves 2%.
- **Budgets:** stop when 20% of T has been edited. The text is saved after 2%, 5%, 10% and 20%, decoded to a string, which is what the owner receives (the owner re-tokenises it). (Study 2's rule judges edits at 10% (decision 6); the pilot reports every budget, so that the judgement point can be reviewed before the protocol.)
- **E-rand:** the same rounds, budgets, whole-word rule and plausibility limit; positions drawn uniformly from the eligible ones; the replacement drawn uniformly from the eligible candidates (seeded). One unsteered pass per round.
- **Pilot stand-in for E-est:** the tuning keys have no Study 1 estimate. The stand-in is a 4-sparse vector at layer 14 that shares the key's smallest-magnitude support coordinate (same value) and has 3 random other coordinates with uniform[−1, 1] values (seeded), so its cosine with the key is typically 0.0–0.2, like Study 1's estimates (median 0.00–0.16). It serves only for mechanics and timing; its results say nothing about the study's estimates.

### 2.3 The owner's test (unchanged from Study 3)
S4: x = (g − μ_g)/σ_g with pool F's μ and σ; T(x, v) = cos(x, v); exact p-value against `core.null_keys` (M = 999); **detected** if p ≤ 0.01. `power_pilot.text_features`, `transform` and `pvalues` are imported unchanged. (The owner's probe exists only for study keys, so it is not in the pilot.)

### 2.4 Batching
Paraphrases are generated in batches of 8 texts, sorted by input length (less padding; left padding with the attention mask). The design timing hinted that Phi's batched outputs might be shorter than its unbatched ones (about 160 vs 197 words on average), but those were different texts. A check on the same texts did not finish: Phi ran under heavy swap, and even 8 texts took more than 40 minutes. Validation check 5 (§7) therefore compares, with greedy decoding, the same 8 pool F texts paraphrased in one batch and one at a time, per model. **It passes if the mean word-count ratio (batched / one at a time) is at least 0.90.** If it fails, the batching code is fixed before the pilot runs. If no fix works, I bring options to Yichen: one text at a time would make Phi take about 25 h in Study 2.

## 3. Quality conditions (Study 2's evaluation definitions, decisions 3–4)
For a scrub *z* of an original genuine text *o* with prompt *q*:
- **Fluency:** perplexity of *z*'s first 256 tokens under Qwen2.5-1.5B given *q* (v0.2's definition, `common.perplexities`, computed in right-padded batches; validation check 4 confirms equality with v0.2's function) ≤ max(1.25 × that of *o*, P95_H(perplexity)); and seq-rep-4 (v0.4's `seqrep4`, unchanged) ≤ max(that of *o*, P95_H(seq-rep-4)). P95_H = the 95th percentile over the **human continuations** of pool A's 1,000 prompts, scored the same way (Yichen, 2026-09-30, revising decision 3: the earlier normal range, the 95th percentile of the model's own texts, was 7.35, and only 6% of human continuations fall below it; on the human continuations, median 12.9 and P95 25.4, from the dry run below).
- **Length:** the Qwen token count of *z* ≥ 0.8 × that of *o* (the continuation strings, without special tokens; *o* counted up to 256).
- **Meaning:** cos(e(z), e(o)) > P95_pairs, where *e* is the all-mpnet-base-v2 embedding (mean pooling over the attention mask, then L2 normalisation, at most 384 tokens, following the model's `modules.json`) and P95_pairs is the 95th percentile over pool A's 1,000 same-prompt pairs (model text vs human continuation).
- **Scrub success:** S4 p > 0.01 **and** all four conditions pass.

## 4. What the pilot reports (descriptive; tuning keys only; no rule depends on detection)
- Per method and strength (median over the 4 tuning keys, and per key): S4 detection before and after; scrub success; the pass rate of each condition and of all four; length ratio; perplexity ratio; cosine. Paraphrase: also first-attempt-only results and the number of attempts used. Edits: everything at 2%, 5%, 10% and 20%, and the attacker's own objective before and after.
- **Calibration after paraphrase:** the 100 unwatermarked tuning texts, paraphrased by each model with the same procedure, tested against the 4 tuning keys (pooled FPR over 400 tests) and against 1,000 fresh keys from the public key distribution (seeds disjoint from all others): the mean per-key FPR and the share of keys above 3%. Descriptive only; Study 2's binding gates use pool A's 1,000 texts.
- **The bar values:** P95_H(perplexity), P95_H(seq-rep-4), P95_pairs, and the attacker's pool C counterparts. **Stricter readings (secondary):** scrub success with the normal range taken as the 95th percentile of pool A's model texts (decision 3's original bar) and as the median of the human continuations.
- **Samples read:** for each method at ρ = 0.50, the first text of keys 9001 and 9002 (original and scrub; edits at the 10% budget), quoted in the report.
- **Timing** per text, per method and stage, and the projected Study 2 run time (check I5).

## 5. Implementation checks (must pass before the Study 2 protocol is drafted; a failure is fixed and the pilot re-run as v0.2)
- **I1, contributions:** on the 25 watermarked texts of key 9001 at ρ = 0.50, Σ_t c_t(x_t) for the true-key direction against the autograd derivative ⟨g, ŵ⟩: Pearson r ≥ 0.99 (code test with a random direction on unwatermarked texts: r = 0.996, slope 1.005); and per token, c_t at s and at s/2 agree (Spearman, median over the first 10 texts, ≥ 0.8), so single-token contributions are not rounding noise (code test: 0.86–0.92).
- **I2, the edits act on the key:** over the 400 watermarked texts, the change in the owner's T(x, v) from 0% to 10% is more negative under E-true than under E-rand (paired Wilcoxon, p < 0.01), and E-true's median change is negative. (This checks the direction, not the strength: how far edits go is what Study 2 measures.)
- **I3, edit bookkeeping:** every hook is removed after each pass (logits equal the unhooked logits); each saved token sequence differs from the original at exactly the budgeted number of positions; after decoding and re-encoding, the median share of tokens that change is ≤ 2%.
- **I4, embeddings:** identical texts give cosine 1 (± 1e-5); batched and one-at-a-time embeddings agree within 1e-4 (padding is masked); pool A's pairs align by prompt (the same prompt ID behind the model text and the human continuation).
- **I5, time budget:** the projected Study 2 run (decision 2's grid with pool A's paraphrases and all scoring) is ≤ 24 h. If not, I stop and bring Yichen options (for example fewer texts for the edit arms) before the protocol.

## 6. Adjustments the pilot may make (rules fixed now; no detection result is used)
- **A1, paraphrase prompt:** if, for either paraphraser, fewer than 70% of first attempts on the 400 watermarked texts meet the length floor, the prompt becomes P2 for both paraphrasers: P1 with "Keep it about the same length." replaced by "Your paraphrase must be at least as long as the original text." The ρ = 0.50 texts (100) are then paraphrased again with P2 (with the same self-check), and its length and quality pass rates are reported beside P1's on the same texts. If P2 also fails the 70% mark, I bring it to Yichen.
- **A2, edit round size:** E-true is also run with *r* = 1% and *r* = 5% of T on the ρ = 0.50 texts (100). The chosen *r* is the largest of {1%, 2%, 5%} whose median reduction of the attacker's own objective (⟨g, ŵ⟩ with its σ̂) at the 10% budget is at least 90% of the largest median reduction among the three. This uses only what the attacker can compute. (Comparing with the best of the three, not with *r* = 1%, follows from the dry run: on 4 stand-in texts, 1% rounds reduced the objective less than 2% rounds, and a 1% reference would have picked 5% for the wrong reason.)
Nothing else is tuned: the bars, budgets, candidate count (10), the plausibility limit (1/3), *s*, the self-check and the rules stay as written.

## 7. Validation before the run (inputs only)
A **dry run** of the run and analysis phases (2026-09-30, `--dry`, scratch folder, Qwen only) used only unwatermarked tuning texts from outside the pilot set, relabelled as stand-ins for every set. No watermarked text was read and its numbers carry no information. It executed every code path and exposed the fluency-bar problem (§3) and the A2 reference (§6), both fixed in this draft.

1. Every input file exists and is hashed; the counts are 400 watermarked, 100 unwatermarked, 200 pool F, 1,000 pool A, 200 pool C.
2. The tuning keys rebuild with norm ρ·N and 4 nonzero coordinates; the stand-in estimates' cosines are recorded; all seeds (paraphrase, random edits, stand-ins, the 1,000 fresh keys) are disjoint from every other seed in the project.
3. Pool alignment: pool A's model texts, prompts and human continuations share one prompt order; pool C's references are disjoint from pool A.
4. On 2 unwatermarked tuning texts outside the pilot set (not watermarked ones): one paraphrase attempt per model, one full edit run with a random direction and one random-edit run, and every quality and S4 field produced. This is the smoke test; its numbers are discarded. It also checks I4 (embeddings), that the attacker's gradient at layer 14 equals the Study 3 pilot's, that the hook is removed (logits unchanged), that the edit counts are exact, and that the batched perplexity equals v0.2's function on 20 pool A texts (maximum relative difference ≤ 1e-3; code test: 0).
5. **Batching** (§2.4): greedy decoding, the same 8 pool F texts, one batch of 8 vs one at a time, per model; the mean word-count ratio must be at least 0.90.
6. Timing of each stage, projected to the pilot (budget: 4 h).
