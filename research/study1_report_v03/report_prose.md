## 4. EXPLORATORY (added after the run; not pre-registered): perplexity-based fluency admits repetitive text
**Why.** The first pre-registered sample (Route B, key 1001, ρ = 0.50, layer 1; §5 below) is visibly degenerate ("can - can can I have I've had …"), yet its perplexity is below the fluency bar and the probe accepts it. Repetition *lowers* perplexity, so a perplexity ceiling cannot catch it.
**Diagnostic (chosen after seeing that sample).** rep3 = 1 − distinct word 3-grams / all word 3-grams. A text is "repetitive" if its rep3 exceeds the 95th percentile of its key-level's oracle texts (mirroring the fluency rule). FA_nr = the share of texts accepted, fluent and not repetitive. Median over 8 keys. No rule uses this.

<<EXPLORATORY>>

**Reading.**
- At **ρ = 0.70, Route B's FA of 69% is almost all repetitive text**: 80–90% of its fluent-accepted texts are flagged, and FA_nr falls to 3.5–4.5%, against 90.0% for the oracle.
- Route A at 0.70 keeps more: FA_nr is 43.0% at n = 256, against 70.5% FA.
- At **ρ = 0.35**, the one level where F1 says "practical", Route B's FA_nr (12.5% at n = 256) is only a little below its FA (15.0%). The practical verdict is therefore not mainly an artefact of repetition, but its narrow margin would not survive this extra condition (the F1 bar is 14.5%).
- The random-key control also loses FA to repetition at 0.70 (47.5% to 37.5%).

**What reading the texts shows.** Flagged texts often start fluently and fall into a loop later (for example key 1004, ρ = 0.70, Route B, text 0 ends by repeating "so now let me tell you that the list of the possible free agents in the NBA …"). Some unflagged texts drift off the prompt into an unrelated chat format ("Assistant: Here is an explanation …"). rep3 catches looping, not drift. Neither is measured by the pre-registered rule.
**Inference.** The owner's probe accepts steered text largely for its off-distribution activations, as v0.2 suggested. A perplexity-only attacker check then selects low layers whose steering produces low-perplexity loops. A fluency condition for forgery needs a repetition (diversity) term as well as perplexity, and ideally a judgment of semantic coherence.

## 7. What this means for the paper (inference; claim gate pending)
- Supported: passive footprint forgery without the key produces texts the owner's probe accepts, and at the weakest working strength (ρ = 0.35) a perplexity-screened forgery meets the pre-registered bar for fluent forgery.
- Not supported as a pre-registered finding: fluent forgery at ρ = 0.50 or 0.70 (inconclusive), or any claim that the forgeries are high quality beyond perplexity.
- Strongly suggested but exploratory: the probe's acceptance of steered text is largely driven by off-distribution activations, including repetitive loops. A quality condition must include a repetition term.
